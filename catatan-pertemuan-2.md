# Meeting 2 – Models and Taxonomy of Collaboration



## Material Summary



## 1- Time-Space Matrix



The time-space matrix classifies collaboration based on when and where people work together. It has four categories:



* **Same Time, Same Place**: People collaborate face-to-face, such as in classroom or office meetings.
* **Same Time, Different Place**: People collaborate remotely in real time, such as through video conferences and live chats.
* **Different Time, Same Place**: People work at different times in the same location, such as using announcement boards or shared project rooms.
* **Different Time, Different Place**: People collaborate remotely at different times, such as through email, forums, wikis, Git, and GitHub.



## 2- Synchronous and Asynchronous Communication



There are two main types of communication:

* **Synchronous**: Communication happens in real time, allowing immediate feedback. It is suitable for brainstorming and quick decisions. Examples include video calls, live chats, and collaborative editing.
* **Asynchronous**: Communication allows people to respond at different times and work according to their own schedules. Examples include email, forums, wikis, and pull requests.



A good practice is to combine both methods: use synchronous communication for discussions and decisions, and asynchronous communication for documentation.



## 3- Types of Groupware



Groupware refers to software that supports teamwork and collaboration. Its main types include:



* **Communication**: Email, chat, and forums for exchanging messages.
* **Conferencing**: Video and audio conferences, webinars, and screen sharing.
* **Collaborative Editing**: Creating and editing documents, slides, and designs together.
* **Project Management**: Kanban boards, issue trackers, and workflows for organizing tasks.
* **Version Control**: Git and hosting platforms for managing and tracking changes in team code.
* **Decision Support**: Polling, voting, and digital whiteboards to help teams make decisions.



## 4- Key Features of Collaborative Systems



Collaborative systems have several important features:



* **Shared Workspace**: Allows authorized team members to access and modify the same files or documents.
* **Awareness**: Shows who is online and what other members are currently working on.
* **Access Control**: Uses roles and permissions, such as read, write, and admin, to maintain security.
* **Notifications and History**: Provides change notifications and version history to track changes and restore previous work.



## 5- Centralized and Peer-to-Peer Architecture



Collaborative systems use two main architectures:



* **Centralized (Client-Server)**: A central server manages data and acts as the main source of information. It is easier to manage but can become a single point of failure.
* **Peer-to-Peer (Distributed)**: Each participant keeps their own copy of the data and exchanges changes with others. It is more resilient, but synchronization is more complicated.
